## EntropyLab

A single HTML file that never generates entropy ([issue #20](https://github.com/reardencode/seattle_bitdevs/issues/20)). You bring dice, cards, coins, or hex; it derives BIP39, xpubs, multisig, silent payments, and PSBTs offline, and runs a live χ² check on the die as you roll. Download the file and open it on an air-gapped machine.

* [entropylab.online](https://entropylab.online/) — download the HTML; do not type secrets on a networked machine

## Encrypted descriptors (BIP138) and wallet label sync

Losing the descriptor is almost as bad as losing the seed: the seed signs, the descriptor maps you to the coins. BIP138 encrypts descriptors, wallet policies, and labels to the lexicographically sorted xpubs in the payload, so any designated cosigner can decrypt from their own key and an untrusted cloud copy leaks neither addresses nor script shape. ChaCha20-Poly1305, decoy secrets to hide the quorum, optional size padding. Private keys must be stripped first. Liana's older `BEB` magic is not compatible.

Separately, Jakub's label-sync draft stores encrypted BIP329 labels derived from a canonical descriptor (no private keys), last-write-wins, with a Nostr reference implementation. Bitcoin Safe already does this; Craig Raw wants a broader spec.

* [BIP138](https://github.com/bitcoin/bips/blob/master/bip-0138.md) — [bips.dev](https://bips.dev/138/)
* [Delving: a simple backup scheme](https://delvingbitcoin.org/t/a-simple-backup-scheme-for-wallet-accounts/1607)
* [Rust implementation](https://github.com/pythcoiner/bip138)

## October incidents: implants, fake RBF, trampolines, Ark

A month of thefts that are all the same lesson: the device, the server, or the invoice parser lied, and the wallet signed what it was shown.

* **Ledger Nano X implant.** Some units sold through CryptoBilis (and some grey-market units Tibane tore down) carried an extra board on the screen bus that OCR'd the 24-word backup and sent it over LTE. Genuine Check does not see the implant. Buy from the manufacturer, inspect the board, never type a seed that was shown on a device of unknown origin. Third-party on-chain estimates ~$86–93M across ~300 wallets; Ledger has not confirmed a total. [Tibane teardown](https://www.tibane.net/research/ledger-nano-x-implant) · [Karpelès](https://x.com/MagicalTux/status/2108576506704548179) · [Ledger Support](https://x.com/Ledger_Support/status/2108551100613714002) · [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/ledger-investigates-fund-losses-linked-to-southeast-asian-reseller-warns-users)
* **Electrum fake RBF.** A malicious server swallows the tx you just broadcast and feeds the wallet a conflicting, **unsigned** replacement whose change address matches the last four characters of yours. Light clients do not check mempool signatures. If you then bump the fee, you sign the attacker's outputs. Happening in the wild. Upgrade to **4.8.3**. Running your own server is not affected. [GHSA-6jp4-8v48-cc5v](https://github.com/spesmilo/electrum/security/advisories/GHSA-6jp4-8v48-cc5v)
* **Eclair trampoline theft.** A trampoline retry that relaxed CLTV limits let a payer reclaim the incoming HTLC after the outgoing one was claimed. Fixed in 0.14.5. [Eclair 0.14.5](https://github.com/ACINQ/eclair/releases/tag/v0.14.5) · [Optech #428](https://bitcoinops.org/en/newsletters/2026/10/23/#eclair-3409)
* **BOLT11 duplicate `p`.** Invoice readers must reject a BOLT11 unless it contains **exactly one** payment hash (`p`) and one payment secret (`s`). LNP2PBot's parser and node picked different hashes; attackers collected duplicate payouts. [BOLTs #1357](https://github.com/lightning/bolts/issues/1357) · [LND #11190](https://github.com/lightningnetwork/lnd/pull/11190)
* **Second's Ark, 0.75 BTC.** On October 5 an attacker registered Bark boards with only their own signatures, exited those boards, and spent the same VTXOs out of Second's node over Lightning. Operator liquidity; user VTXOs sit on pre-signed exits the server cannot spend. First mainnet Ark operator exploit. [Second thread](https://x.com/secondhq/status/2108065465532846282) · [Stacker News](https://stacker.news/items/1591231)

## Bitcoin Core 32.1 and the disclosure policy

v32.0 was tagged October 21 and never shipped. A remotely triggerable TCP crash landed on the 23rd; **32.1** shipped October 27 with that fix and two others, plus 31.2, 30.4, and 29.5. **29.x is EOL.** Easily discoverable bugs (the bar LLM scans now clear) skip the old embargo: patch every maintained branch and publish the advisory the same day. Three of those went out this week. Three medium/high bugs fixed in 30.0 still follow the regular clock and land in two weeks.

## rust-bitcoin summit (Nashville)

The rust-bitcoin project met in Nashville the week after TABConf, October 19–21. Poelstra had already given "The Road to 1.0" in Atlanta, and that was a main topic of the summit. Many crates are already 1.0 and work continues on the rest. There was much discussion of the challenges of dependency management and gradual upgrades within the rust dependency system, with the rust-bitcoin project taking an innovative approach of embedding old and new types within the old crates to enable migration. One live topic at the summit was rbitcoin: Matt Corallo said he is giving up on esplora, mempool-esplora, and electrs and supporting rbitcoin only; Brandon's take is that rbitcoin will be the reference Esplora implementation before it is the reference consensus implementation. Spiral put Renato Britto on rust-bitcoin 1.0 crates full-time (units-first, BDK off 0.32, fuzzers and mutants before stable).

## Co-signer spending policies

Luke Carriere's draft standardizes the **policy** a co-signer enforces, not the co-signer. Open, blinded, and enclave verification; conditions from the transaction or from DLC oracle attestations; anything Script can already enforce stays in Script; every policy keeps a path that needs no co-signer. Aimed at the zoo of Liquidium / Lendasat / bonded co-signer products that currently cannot share a wallet.

## Silent payments for multiparty wallets

BIP-352 skipped FROST and MuSig2 inputs for lack of a proof. Oleks's Delving post (October 15) gives one: each signer contributes a weighted ECDH share, **AggDLEQ** folds those into a single BIP-374 proof in two rounds, and the coordinator writes ordinary BIP-375 fields. Compatible with BIP-352/374/375/327/445; no extra PSBT fields. The interesting assumption is DH-AOMDL (AOMDL plus a static DH helper), and its hardness is open. macgyver has a two-round `PSBT_IN_SP_PARTIAL_*` prototype on Jade, COLDCARD, and Frostsnap that publishes a per-signer proof instead of aggregating.

* [Delving](https://delvingbitcoin.org/t/silent-payments-for-multiparty-wallets/2960)

## Descriptor syntax for hash preimages

jaonoctus extends BIP379 with `sha256(preimage(HEX))` (and the other hash types). The preimage is always 32 bytes. A public descriptor prints the digest; a private one holds the preimage like a WIF. This is for keys and scripts that *commit* to a preimage at descriptor time, not for spend-time HTLCs. Salvatore's note: a literal preimage is spent once, then shared across a ranged `/*`. The syntax came out of a TABConf Capture the Bitcoin challenge.

* [Delving](https://delvingbitcoin.org/t/descriptor-syntax-for-hash-preimages/2935)

## Lichen and LN-GAP

Adam Gibson's two-part off-chain contract work. **LN-GAP** runs multi-round two-party programs (chess is the demo) inside a Lightning channel; a venue attests *when* a move was published, not what it said, and an honest-majority of one-bit `OP_CHECKSIGADD` signatures settles disputes in a constant number of on-chain txs. **Lichen** is a single-issuer L2: each user has a 2-of-2 with the hub, the sequencer runs the ledger, and a disputed Groth16/BitVMX path lets the user exit without the hub. The sequencer can still omit a return.

* [LN-GAP paper](https://github.com/AdamISZ/ln-gap/blob/master/docs/paper/lngap-short.pdf) — [repo](https://github.com/AdamISZ/ln-gap) — [mailing list](https://groups.google.com/g/bitcoindev/c/pjv6bL_42kg)

## Cassis: multi-layer HTLC hops

If a layer can express an HTLC, it can be a hop. andunie's scheme (October 8) chains HTLCs across Lightning, Liquid, Cashu, Fedimint, Arkade, Rootstock, and on-chain, all locked to the same SHA-256 hash, timeouts decreasing toward the receiver. A routing node is anyone with funds in two layers. Instant and cheap depending on the path; each user picks custody and privacy by picking a layer and can still pay anyone else. Open problems: Iroh JSON for comms, Nostr announcements, reputation so routers do not lock funds they later have to reclaim, invoice format, offline receive. Experimental Rust at cassiscash/cassis.

* [Delving](https://delvingbitcoin.org/t/cassis-a-new-simple-scheme-for-generic-multi-layer-multi-hop-htlc-routing/2952)
* [cassis.cash](https://cassis.cash/)
* [GitHub](https://github.com/cassiscash/cassis)

## BIP324 one-byte aliases

BIP324's one-byte message IDs are a global table, so a new message needs coordination or the full 12-byte name. aj's draft lets each peer send `set324alias` at connect time and pick its own one-byte IDs for the messages **it** sends. Savings: up to 66% on a ping-only link, ~5% typical, zero until new IDs exist.

## QSAVE

A "don't burn, don't freeze" path for coins that never migrate. After a height, a legacy spend also needs a second factor (a seed-derivation ZK proof, or a watermark committed before the cutoff); without it the coins can only move to a recovery output. No sunset. Alternative to BIP-361-style freezes for the long tail, including P2PK.

* [Mailing list](https://groups.google.com/g/bitcoindev/c/5G38b3JL9fA)
