## Binance hot wallets drained

This afternoon, billions left Binance hot wallets. On-chain counts are still moving: roughly $3–6 billion depending on who is counting, across Ethereum, Bitcoin, and Tron, including about 18,270 BTC. Tether and Circle froze on the order of $2.5 billion in USDT and USDC. Nobody can freeze the bitcoin. CZ says it looks like a hack and that user funds will be covered. Binance is investigating, has paused withdrawals, and says user funds are safe. It has not published a final loss number. One on-chain story is that Binance 14's own key signed an EIP-7702 delegation, after which a single transaction swept dozens of tokens. The previous record was Bybit in 2025, about $1.5 billion.

## October incidents: implants, fake RBF, trampolines, Ark

October was a month of thefts with the same shape: the device, the server, or the invoice parser lied, and the wallet signed what it was shown.

* **Ledger Nano X implant.** Some units sold through CryptoBilis (and some grey-market units Tibane tore down) carried an extra board on the screen bus that OCR'd the 24-word backup and sent it over LTE. Genuine Check does not see the implant. Buy from the manufacturer, inspect the board, never type a seed that was shown on a device of unknown origin. Third-party on-chain estimates ~$86–93M across ~300 wallets; Ledger has not confirmed a total. [Tibane teardown](https://www.tibane.net/research/ledger-nano-x-implant) · [Karpelès](https://x.com/MagicalTux/status/2108576506704548179) · [Ledger Support](https://x.com/Ledger_Support/status/2108551100613714002) · [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/ledger-investigates-fund-losses-linked-to-southeast-asian-reseller-warns-users)
* **Electrum fake RBF.** A malicious server swallows the tx you just broadcast and feeds the wallet a conflicting, **unsigned** replacement whose change address matches the last four characters of yours. Light clients do not check mempool signatures. If you then bump the fee, you sign the attacker's outputs. Happening in the wild. Upgrade to **4.8.3**. Running your own server is not affected. [GHSA-6jp4-8v48-cc5v](https://github.com/spesmilo/electrum/security/advisories/GHSA-6jp4-8v48-cc5v)
* **Eclair trampoline theft.** A trampoline retry that relaxed CLTV limits let a payer reclaim the incoming HTLC after the outgoing one was claimed. Fixed in 0.14.5. [Eclair 0.14.5](https://github.com/ACINQ/eclair/releases/tag/v0.14.5) · [Optech #428](https://bitcoinops.org/en/newsletters/2026/10/23/#eclair-3409)
* **BOLT11 duplicate `p`.** Invoice readers must reject a BOLT11 unless it contains **exactly one** payment hash (`p`) and one payment secret (`s`). LNP2PBot's parser and node picked different hashes; attackers collected duplicate payouts. [BOLTs #1357](https://github.com/lightning/bolts/issues/1357) · [LND #11190](https://github.com/lightningnetwork/lnd/pull/11190)
* **Second's Ark, 0.75 BTC.** On October 5 an attacker registered Bark boards with only their own signatures, exited those boards, and spent the same VTXOs out of Second's node over Lightning. Operator liquidity; user VTXOs sit on pre-signed exits the server cannot spend. First mainnet Ark operator exploit. [Second thread](https://x.com/secondhq/status/2108065465532846282) · [Stacker News](https://stacker.news/items/1591231)

## rust-bitcoin summit (Nashville)

The rust-bitcoin project met in Nashville the week after TABConf, October 19–21. Poelstra had already given "The Road to 1.0" in Atlanta, and that was a main topic of the summit. Many crates are already 1.0 and work continues on the rest. There was much discussion of the challenges of dependency management and gradual upgrades within the rust dependency system, with the rust-bitcoin project taking an innovative approach of embedding old and new types within the old crates to enable migration. One live topic at the summit was rbitcoin: Matt Corallo said he is giving up on esplora, mempool-esplora, and electrs and supporting rbitcoin only; Brandon's take is that rbitcoin will be the reference Esplora implementation before it is the reference consensus implementation. Spiral put Renato Britto on rust-bitcoin 1.0 crates full-time (units-first, BDK off 0.32, fuzzers and mutants before stable).

## EntropyLab

EntropyLab is one HTML file, and it never invents entropy for you ([issue #20](https://github.com/reardencode/seattle_bitdevs/issues/20)). You bring dice, cards, coins, or hex. It turns that into BIP39, xpubs, multisig, silent payments, and PSBTs, all offline, and it runs a live χ² check on the die as you roll. Download the file and open it on a machine that is not on the network.

* [entropylab.online](https://entropylab.online/) — download the HTML; do not type secrets on a networked machine

## Bitcoin Core 32.1 and the disclosure policy

Core tagged v32.0 on October 21 and never shipped it. A remotely triggerable TCP crash landed two days later. **32.1** went out October 27 with that fix and two others, plus 31.2, 30.4, and 29.5. **29.x is EOL.** The disclosure policy changed too: bugs that an LLM scan can find skip the old embargo. You patch every maintained branch and publish the advisory the same day. Three of those went out this week. ## Encrypted descriptors (BIP138) and wallet label sync

If you lose the descriptor, you can still have the seed and not find the coins. The seed signs; the descriptor is the map. BIP138 encrypts that map — descriptors, wallet policies, and labels — to the xpubs sitting in the payload, sorted so any of the designated cosigners can open it from their own key. An untrusted cloud copy then leaks neither addresses nor the shape of the script. It uses ChaCha20-Poly1305, decoy secrets to hide the quorum, and optional size padding. Strip the private keys first. Liana's older `BEB` magic will not open these.

On labels, Jakub's draft stores encrypted BIP329 labels derived from a canonical descriptor, with no private keys in the blob, last-write-wins, and a Nostr reference implementation. Bitcoin Safe already does this. Craig Raw wants a broader spec.

* [BIP138](https://github.com/bitcoin/bips/blob/master/bip-0138.md) — [bips.dev](https://bips.dev/138/)
* [Delving: a simple backup scheme](https://delvingbitcoin.org/t/a-simple-backup-scheme-for-wallet-accounts/1607)

## BIP324 one-byte aliases

BIP324's one-byte message IDs live in a global table. A new message means coordinating a slot, or falling back to the full 12-byte name. aj's draft lets each peer send `set324alias` at connect time and pick its own one-byte IDs for the messages **it** sends. A ping-only link can save up to 66%. Typical traffic is closer to 5%. You get nothing until new IDs exist.

## Co-signer spending policies

Luke Carriere's draft is about the **policy** a co-signer enforces, not about who the co-signer is. You can verify that policy in the open, blinded, or in an enclave. Conditions can come from the transaction itself or from DLC oracle attestations. Anything Script already enforces stays in Script. Every policy keeps a path that does not need a co-signer at all. The point is the current zoo of Liquidium / Lendasat / bonded co-signer products cannot share a wallet today.

## Silent payments for multiparty wallets

BIP-352 skipped FROST and MuSig2 inputs because there was no security proof. Oleks posted one on Delving (October 15). Each signer contributes a weighted ECDH share. **AggDLEQ** folds those into a single BIP-374 proof in two rounds, and the coordinator writes ordinary BIP-375 fields. It lines up with BIP-352, 374, 375, 327, and 445, and it does not add PSBT fields. The interesting assumption is DH-AOMDL — AOMDL plus a static DH helper — and nobody has shown that is hard. macgyver has a two-round `PSBT_IN_SP_PARTIAL_*` prototype on Jade, COLDCARD, and Frostsnap that publishes a per-signer proof instead of aggregating.

* [Delving](https://delvingbitcoin.org/t/silent-payments-for-multiparty-wallets/2960)

## Descriptor syntax for hash preimages

jaonoctus wants BIP379 to grow a private form for hash fragments: `sha256(preimage(HEX))`, and the same for the other hash types. The preimage is always 32 bytes. A public descriptor prints the digest; a private one holds the preimage the way a WIF holds a key. This is for scripts that already know the preimage when you write the descriptor. Spend-time HTLCs still belong in PSBT fields. Salvatore's note: a literal preimage is spent once, then every other output in a ranged `/*` shares it. The syntax came out of a TABConf Capture the Bitcoin challenge.

* [Delving](https://delvingbitcoin.org/t/descriptor-syntax-for-hash-preimages/2935)

## Lichen and LN-GAP

Adam Gibson posted two pieces of off-chain contract work. **LN-GAP** runs a multi-round two-party program — chess is the demo — inside a Lightning channel. A venue attests *when* a move was published, not what it said. An honest-majority of one-bit `OP_CHECKSIGADD` signatures settles disputes in a constant number of on-chain transactions. **Lichen** is a single-issuer L2. Each user has a 2-of-2 with the hub, the sequencer runs the ledger, and a disputed Groth16/BitVMX path lets the user exit without the hub. The sequencer can still omit a return.

## Cassis: multi-layer HTLC hops

If a layer can express an HTLC, it can be a hop. That is andunie's scheme (October 8). You chain HTLCs across Lightning, Liquid, Cashu, Fedimint, Arkade, Rootstock, and on-chain, all locked to the same SHA-256 hash, with timeouts getting shorter toward the receiver. A routing node is anyone with funds in two layers. Depending on the path, a payment can be instant and cheap. You pick a layer for custody and privacy and you can still pay anyone else. The open problems are the boring ones: Iroh JSON for comms, Nostr announcements, reputation so routers do not lock funds they later have to reclaim, invoice format, and receiving while offline. There is experimental Rust at cassiscash/cassis.

* [Delving](https://delvingbitcoin.org/t/cassis-a-new-simple-scheme-for-generic-multi-layer-multi-hop-htlc-routing/2952)
* [cassis.cash](https://cassis.cash/)
* [GitHub](https://github.com/cassiscash/cassis)

## QSAVE

QSAVE is a path for coins that never migrate: don't burn them, don't freeze them. After a height, a legacy spend also needs a second factor — a seed-derivation ZK proof, or a watermark committed before the cutoff. Without that, the coins can only move to a recovery output. There is no sunset. It is an alternative to BIP-361-style freezes for the long tail, including P2PK.
