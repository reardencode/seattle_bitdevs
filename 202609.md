## CLARITY Act: cloture fails

On September 15 the Senate voted 50–49 to take up the Digital Asset Market Clarity Act, ten short of the 60 votes cloture requires, and the bill is stalled into the election recess. Moran, Collins, Hawley, and Tillis joined every Democrat; Bitcoin’s own classification is unchanged, and what failed is a statute for exchanges and intermediaries.

## Brevo phishing: fake firmware alerts

On September 9 an attacker used a SAML SSO scoping bug at newsletter vendor Brevo to send mail from real customer accounts, including Trezor, BitBox, and CoinTracking. The hardware-wallet messages claimed an STM32 or microcontroller entropy bug and linked an app that asked for the wallet backup; Trezor’s went to about 347,000 addresses, about 2,500 people opened the link before the domain was pulled, and a seed is at risk only if it was typed into that app.

* [Trezor](https://trezor.io/blog/news/security-incident-at-brevo-our-third-party-email-provider)

## Alby Hub: public management API

Hub v1.7.0 through v1.18.5 can be taken over when the management API is reachable from the internet; Alby says one user was affected and that v1.19.0 (August 2025) and later are not. If an old Hub was public, close the port, upgrade to v1.24.0, and change the unlock password.

## rbitcoin 0.7.0

## Liquid: rangeproof cache inflation

On September 6 a rangeproof-cache collision in Elements let one transaction mint ~4,000 unbacked LBTC; SideSwap pegged it out within the hour and the reserve fell from ~4,205 BTC to 197 BTC. A September 1 patch for a 2018 cache bug concatenated variable-length fields with no length prefix, so a longer proof plus a shorter script reused a cached “valid” entry; keys and the CT math were fine, 3,400 BTC came back the next day, ~602 BTC is still out, and peg-outs stay paused on Elements v23.3.4.

## CLN: ping flood OOM

A peer that completes the Noise handshake, requests maximum-size `pong`s, and never reads can grow `connectd`’s output queue until the node is OOM-killed — no channel required, because locally handled messages skipped the read backpressure gate. The fix shipped in v25.09; the public writeup is from August 25, and current nodes should be on 26.06.7 or newer.

* [Delving](https://delvingbitcoin.org/t/disclosure-crashing-cln-with-a-flood-of-pings/2846)
* [Writeup](https://erickcestari.dev/blog/ping-flood-oom/)

## LND forgot collaborative closes after one confirmation

Before v20.0, LND forgot a collaboratively closed channel after one confirmation, so a one-block reorg let a peer publish a revoked commitment with no penalty transaction. The bug was fixed in February, disclosed in August, and nobody is known to have lost funds; the BOLTs say to wait six confirmations.

* [Delving](https://delvingbitcoin.org/t/disclosure-lnd-doesnt-wait-for-enough-confirmations-when-closing-channels/2800)
* [Fix, LND #10331](https://github.com/lightningnetwork/lnd/pull/10331)
* [Optech #419](https://bitcoinops.org/en/newsletters/2026/08/21/)

## `bip322` crate accepts the wrong key

`bip322` 0.0.6–0.0.10 accepts a proof signed by an unrelated key as ownership of any P2WPKH or P2SH-P2WPKH address, because it compared the witness pubkey to itself. P2TR is unaffected; upgrade to 0.0.11, and there is no workaround.

* [GHSA-5chw-87w3-j9cv](https://github.com/rust-bitcoin/bip322/security/advisories/GHSA-5chw-87w3-j9cv)

## Silent payments: coinbase and light clients

A pool can pay miners with BIP352 inside the coinbase so the block does not list who was paid; that needs two BIP352 changes, and test vectors are up. BlindBit Oracle v2, benchmarked from taproot activation through block 965,089, downloads about 2.1× a taproot-only filter plus tweaks and skips false-positive block fetches, but a client still cannot tell if the server omitted a tweak.

* [Delving: coinbase](https://delvingbitcoin.org/t/silent-payments-coinbase/2833)

## Opt-in replay protection

Splitting coins off the BIP-110 chain was harder than it should have been, so moonsettler proposes a taproot annex that commits a signature to one chain’s block. gmaxwell and Sjors both want that block at least ~100 deep, matching coinbase maturity, so a short reorg cannot invalidate an ordinary payment; Sjors’ prototype uses a new segwit version and about 1.75 extra vbytes.

* [Delving](https://delvingbitcoin.org/t/universal-opt-in-replay-protection/2792)
* [Sjors prototype](https://github.com/Sjors/bitcoin/pull/128)
* [Optech #420](https://bitcoinops.org/en/newsletters/2026/08/28/)

## Babilonia

Adam Gibson’s Babilonia makes a coinjoin look like a two-party bet — shared funding, a refund, and an adaptor-signed payout — so the privacy step is not an obvious coinjoin. Repeated rounds should leave the average user near their starting balance minus fees; v2 splits each bet into unequal pieces because v1 leaked the stake as an integer multiple, and there is still no published privacy metric.

* [Optech #422](https://bitcoinops.org/en/newsletters/2026/09/11/#a-protocol-for-probabilistic-coinjoin-and-covert-betting)

## Utreexo IBD without deletion proofs

Utreexo inclusion proofs during initial sync are on the order of a terabyte, and explicit deletion proofs are still ~200 GB even with caching. Davidson Souza uses a SwiftSync hints file to skip inserting outputs that are already spent, so IBD proof overhead drops near zero and clients resume normal add/delete once they reach the tip.

* [Optech #423](https://bitcoinops.org/en/newsletters/2026/09/18/#improvements-in-utreexo-initial-block-download)

## BIP-54 bounds chain growth

sipa shows both Great Consensus Cleanup timewarp rules are required: at the September 9 tip the proven chain-length cap is about 1,012,794 blocks, and either rule alone leaves a cap in the billions. One formula is machine-checked in Lean, and `getblocktemplate` now applies the Murch–Zawy timestamp floor on the last block of a difficulty period without changing consensus validation.

* [Delving](https://delvingbitcoin.org/t/bounds-on-chain-length-with-bip-54-timewarp-fixes/2899)
* [Core #35949](https://github.com/bitcoin/bitcoin/pull/35949)

## Shielded Bitcoin

[[alloc] init] (Clara Shikhelman, Misha Komarov, Aleksei Moskvin) published a metaprotocol for Zcash-style private notes on Bitcoin L1: encrypted amounts, public nullifiers, and ZK proofs, with state rebuilt by anyone running a node plus an indexer. No soft fork, operator, or sidechain; the paper covers transfers inside the pool, and the trustless peg (PIPEs v2 witness encryption) is still unfinished.

* [Whitepaper](https://www.allocinit.xyz/uploads/shielded-bitcoin.pdf)

## Iceberg: threshold custody on Lightning

Iceberg nests a t-of-n threshold scheme inside one side of MuSig2, so the counterparty still sees an ordinary two-party signer and nothing in Bitcoin or the BOLTs has to change. A prototype on a production node kept over 93% of payment throughput while tolerating one corrupted signer.

* [ePrint 2026/1757](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1757)
* [Nadav Kohen](https://nkohen.github.io/blog/iceberg/)

## PQLN

PQLN is a hybrid post-quantum extension for Lightning’s off-chain messages, implemented in rust-lightning and measured on real nodes. It does not replace on-chain secp256k1; it covers channel state and gossip that a later cryptographically relevant quantum computer could forge.

* [Delving](https://delvingbitcoin.org/t/pqln-post-quantum-security-for-the-bitcoin-lightning-networks-off-chain-surfaces/2893)
